Why this contest

Every block has a story

Many blocks were built by Black-owned businesses. Some stood for a century. Some opened last year. Some are gone, and only the building is left to remember them.

The Enduring Families Project brings Black history to young readers through books like Lifting As We Rise, the story of the Black Women's Clubs. Many of those clubwomen were business owners whose shops helped fund schools, libraries and whole communities. Built on Our Block carries that legacy forward, one doorway at a time.

Who started it? What did it mean to the neighborhood? What is its legacy now? Go find out, and show us.

How to enter

Three steps from storefront to submission

  1. Pick a business

    Choose a Black-owned business, historic (closed now, or a historic site) or current (open today).

  2. Film on location

    Shoot 2 minutes or less. The real place must be on camera: the storefront, the building, or the site where it stood.

  3. Submit

    Upload your video to YouTube or Vimeo (public or unlisted, not private), then fill out the short entry form on this page.

Categories

Rules & terms

The fine print, in plain words

The real location must be in your video.

Film on location and show the actual place: the business, its storefront or building, or the site where it stood. Archival photos may be added, but videos made only from photos or interviews filmed elsewhere do not qualify.

Eligibility
  1. Open to storytellers of all ages in four categories: Young Storytellers (8–12), Teen Storytellers (13–17), Adult / Community (18+) and Group.
  2. Entrants under 18 must submit through a parent, guardian or teacher, who accepts these rules on the entrant's behalf.
  3. Entry is free. Each entrant or group may submit up to 2 videos.
Video requirements
  1. Videos must be 2 minutes or less, including credits.
  2. Each video must be filmed on location and clearly show the actual place: the business itself, its storefront or building, or, for a business that has closed, the site where it stood.
  3. All footage must be original and filmed by the entrant or their team. Photos, music or clips from other sources must be original, royalty-free or properly licensed.
  4. Videos must be family-friendly. No profanity, violence, or hateful or disrespectful content.
  5. Information about the business should be true to the best of the entrant's knowledge. Name your sources (family stories, interviews, library or archive records) on the entry form.
  6. Upload your video to YouTube or Vimeo and set it to Public or Unlisted (not Private) so the judges can watch it. Keep it posted until winners are announced.
  7. Winners must send EFP the original video file within 14 days if asked, so it can be shared and archived.
  8. Be respectful when filming. Ask before filming on private property or inside a business, and get permission from anyone you film.
Rights and use
  1. Entrants keep ownership of their videos.
  2. By submitting, entrants give the Enduring Families Project: Children's Stories permission to share the video, its title and the entrant's name (first name and last initial for entrants under 18) on EFP's website, social media and in educational materials, with credit, at no cost.
Judging and prizes
  1. Entries are judged on storytelling, connection to the business and its community, creativity, and respect for the history shared. Production budget is not a factor.
  2. Cash prizes are awarded from all entries across all categories: First Place $100, Second Place $75, Third Place $50.
  3. Honors (Legacy Award, Still Standing Award, Young Voices Award) carry no cash prize.
  4. For entrants under 18 and Group entries, prizes are paid to the parent, guardian, teacher, group leader or organization that submitted the entry.
  5. Winners must reply within 14 days of notification to claim their prize, or an alternate winner may be chosen.
  6. Winners may need to provide tax information (such as a W-9) before a prize is paid.
  7. Judges' decisions are final.
General
  1. EFP may disqualify any entry that breaks these rules or contains false information.
  2. EFP may update these rules if needed. Any changes will be posted on this page.
  3. Questions? Email info@efpcs.org.

Questions

Before you hit record

Something not covered here? Email info@efpcs.org.

Do I need fancy equipment?

No. A phone or tablet is enough. We judge the story, not the budget. Film sideways (landscape) and stand close when someone is talking so we can hear them.

The business closed years ago. Can I still enter?

Yes. Film the building or the site where it stood, and tell us what used to be there. Old photos can be added, as long as the real place is on camera too.

Does it have to be in my town?

No. Any Black-owned business, anywhere, as long as you can film there in person.

Can our group make one video together?

Yes. Enter the Group category. The group leader submits the entry.
