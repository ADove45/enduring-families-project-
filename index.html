Stories that were nearly lost. Children who need to hear them.

The Enduring Families Project recovers the African American history of the Coulee Region and puts it in the hands of young readers. This Giving Tuesday, December 1, help us reach more classrooms.

Count me in

"Every child should see the whole tree.

First a few twigs. Then branches. Then the shape of the tree."

Rebecca, Co-founder

Historical research can feel like wandering through a forest. At first you find only a few twigs. Gradually the twigs lead to branches, and eventually enough branches connect that you begin to see the shape of the tree.

For years, the stories of the La Crosse area's early African American residents were scattered twigs: a census line, a newspaper notice, a name on a note card. We have spent years connecting them.

Children of color need to see people who look like them in the history of the place they live. All children need to know that their community's story is larger than a single story.

The giants whose shoulders they stand on

When children learn these names, they recognize the giants who came before them, and see that they are connected to the shoulders they stand on.

George Edwin Taylor
Arrived in La Crosse as a child and, in 1904, became the first Black man to run for President of the United States.

Nathan and Sarah Smith

Already in local classrooms

For our first book, "A Long Way to Find a Home," EFP printed 1,000 storybooks and 6,000 coloring books, free to schools, libraries and community centers. Educators in five regional school districts received free classroom materials after joining our professional development sessions.

What your gift does

Our second book, the story of the 19th-century African American women's movement, is ready for printing. The research is complete. The story is written. What stands between this story and thousands of children is funding.

A gift does more than buy a single book. Classroom sets are used year after year, libraries lend them to generation after generation of families, and teachers reuse the lessons and primary documents. Gifts are received through our fiscal sponsor, the La Crosse County Historical Society.
