In Rebecca's words

Rebecca Mormann-Krieger taught high school history for thirty years, always believing that students learn best when they investigate the past for themselves. We asked her what EFP's work is for.

“Recovering stories that were nearly lost and returning them to the children and communities whose history they helped create.”

Did you know?

When La Crosse was a young frontier town in the 1860s, four of its first five barbers were African American.

One of them was Lydia Moss, who owned her own shop on State Street at a time when it was very unusual for a woman to own a business at all. Barbering became one of the most important trades for La Crosse's early Black families, passed down through generations of the Moss, Birney and Shivers families.

Source: La Crosse County Historical Society
